The plan: trade nothing this week

That is a recommendation, not a dodge. Here is the reasoning, which is itself the kind of thing the Oct 23 deliverable asks for.

Zero trades. Four reasons, in order of weight:

1. Return is not scored. The Competition Guide lists “whether your portfolio makes money during the competition” among the things you are explicitly not evaluated on. There is no scoreboard reason to act.

2. A round trip costs 0.60% of a position. $25 each way, $50 on a typical $8,400 line. Any swap has to be right by more than that before it pays for itself.

3. The portfolio did what it was built to do. JNJ +2.09%, BKNG +1.97%, MDT +1.29%, both Treasuries and BIL flat, while the AI names fell. Selling the half that fell, on the day it fell, dismantles the design.

4. Your own research says not to. The team already concluded lump sum beats averaging about two times in three. Deploying Pile C now, on a forecast, contradicts both that and your own written trigger.

The three decisions that could produce a trade

Medtronic — hold, and rewrite the thesis

The MiniMed exchange offer closed Oct 9 and final results landed Oct 14. Medtronic retains zero MiniMed. The ballot reason for buying it was diabetes, so the reason has left the company.

The real question is not “is it up 9%” — it is “would we buy this today for what it now is?” If yes, hold and write the new thesis down. If the only reason was diabetes, sell.

Recommendation: hold. The note of record already says “its device lines sell steadily through any economy,” which is still true of the post-split company, and that note was rewritten on Oct 7 precisely because somebody caught this. Note also that the +9.19% is partly arithmetic — a split-off retires shares at a discount and lifts the stub — so it should not be read as a pick that worked.

The AI chain — do not trim, write it down

Five of the nine names sit on one chain: GOOG and AMZN spend it, NVDA sells the chips, GEV and ETN supply the power. $48,076.81 at cost, 16% of the account.

Recommendation: do not trim on a one-day move. Two reasons. The cluster has a real internal hedge — GOOG and AMZN benefit if AI capex disappoints, which is why they fell under 2% while the suppliers fell 3–7%. And the weakest name on process grounds (ETN, which got in on a corrected mis-click and has no ballot provenance) is the one whose thesis is strengthening, since the Mobility separation concentrates it toward exactly what you bought it for.

The graded response to this is a paragraph in the IPS, not a sale. There is no written theme or correlation analysis anywhere in the team's work, and a judge will find this concentration in about ninety seconds.

Pile C — hold, and tell Coach P the number

The trigger you wrote on Oct 5 deploys $6,000–$12,000 into VOO when VOO closes 10% below cost. Cost is $712.32, so the level is $641.09. VOO closed $707.43. It is 9.4% away.

Recommendation: hold. His ten-percent correction is roughly what it would take, which means you already have his answer in writing and it is more defensible than acting on the forecast. The message to send him is here.

Two gaps to close at the meeting: the trigger was never voted on, and Trigger 1 cannot physically fire — it needs a buy price written in advance for an approved name, and none was ever written for any of the nine.
