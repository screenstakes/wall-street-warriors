Formula 3 · position weight

How big one stock is in our portfolio

weight = shares × price ÷ total
sharesHow many we ownCaleb's Log or WInS90
priceToday's priceWInS$220
totalEverything we haveWInS, cash included$300,000
  1. 90 × $220 = $19,800
  2. $19,800 ÷ $300,000 = 0.066 = 6.6%

Why it helps us: it checks our limits, 5% when we buy and 8% max. At 6.6% we stop adding.

Formula 4 · shrink money back

What future money is worth today

PV = FV ÷ (1 + r)t
FVMoney we get later$100
rThe return we'd demandThe discount rate, formula 50.10
tYears until we get it3
  1. 1.103 = 1.331
  2. $100 ÷ 1.331 = $75.13

Your Valuation tab calls 1 ÷ (1 + r)t the factor. Year 1 at 9%: 1 ÷ 1.09 = 0.9174.

Why it helps us: a stock is worth all the cash it will make, shrunk back to today. This is the shrinking.

Formula 5 · the discount rate

The return we should demand from a stock

r = rf + β × ERP
rfRisk-free rate10-year Treasury yield, look it up0.045
βHow hard it swings vs the marketYahoo → Statistics. 1 moves with the market0.9
ERPExtra return for owning stocksClass used 5%, same for every stock0.05
  1. 0.9 × 0.05 = 0.045
  2. 0.045 + 0.045 = 0.09 = 9%Example Co's rate. Microsoft in class, β 1.13: about 10%

Why it helps us: a riskier stock has to earn more to be worth it. This sets the bar.

Guess 1 · Laura's growth rate

Where the 7% comes from

You can't look it up. You build it from what each part of the mix has earned:

growth ≈ stock share × stock return + bond share × bond return
stocksUS stocks, long-run averageHistory, not a promise~10%
bondsAbout today's Treasury yield~4%
mixHow we split the moneyHalf and half here50/50
  1. 0.5 × 10% + 0.5 × 4% = 5% + 2% = 7%

Why it helps us: it's a guess, so we always show 5% and 9% too. Pile B runs from $171,704 to $312,157.

Guess 2 · a company's growth

Three ways to pick a stock's growth

  1. Look backUse past free cash flow:
    growth = (last ÷ first)1/years − 1
    2,000 to 2,662 in 3 years: (2,662 ÷ 2,000)1/3 − 1 = 10%
  2. Check analystsYahoo Finance → Analysis: revenue estimates and sales growth for this year and next.
  3. Fade itFast growth doesn't last, so step it down each year. Example Co grows 10%, 9%, 8%, 8%.

If your guess beats both the past and the analysts, the pitch needs a reason.
