How we play it

Sep 15–25

Practice the platform, not the portfolio

Everything in practice is deleted on Sep 25. Use it to learn order entry, how foreign stocks and currency show up, what commissions cost, and what the Rules tab blocks.

Before Sep 28

Write the plan before the first real trade

Judges score how well our strategy fits the client. We keep a shared trade journal from trade one: date, ticker, the reason, which client goal it serves, and the risk. That journal becomes the Oct 23 Trading Notes Analysis.

Sep 28 on

Nobody drops out

If the team falls below 4 members at any point, the whole team is disqualified. If you're not sure you can commit, tell Michael before Sep 25.

All season

Every word we submit is ours

Wharton allows AI tools for brainstorming, but anything AI-generated in a report has to be cited, and plagiarism gets teams dismissed. We write the IPS and final report ourselves.

All season

Everyone owns a piece

Wharton requires every member to contribute, and everyone presents at the semifinals. Each person gets a named role (see the role split below).

All season

No paid help

Paid tutors, education consultants, or outside courses that claim to teach the competition mean automatic disqualification.

What the case study will look like

Every recent case follows the same pattern. A real Wharton alum is the client, but the money situation is made up for the competition. Our team plays analysts trying to win them as a client.

The pattern

  • A dated medium-term goal in dollars, usually 3 to 5 years out.
  • A long goal 10 to 15 years out.
  • An impact or values angle in every case since 2020–21: a foundation, community housing, sustainability.
  • A tension to resolve, like growing the money while paying some of it out.
  • No contacting the client. It's an explicit rule.

What we build first

  • IPS outline: return goal, risk (ability vs. willingness), time horizon for each goal, cash needs and payouts, taxes and legal, the client's values.
  • Trade note template: thesis, client goal served, key numbers (beta, P/E), risk, exit plan.
  • Two buckets: a safer one for the near-term goal and a growth one for the long goal. Both past reports we found used this.
  • Michael's notes: Part Two (growing annuities) covers payout math; Part Seven covers valuing stocks.

Ways to get disqualified

  1. Falling below 4 or going above 6 members at any point in the competition.
  2. Adding members after the official roster is submitted.
  3. Missing deadlines, which makes the team ineligible for the semifinals.
  4. Paid advisors, education consultants, or a non-Wharton course that claims to teach the competition.
  5. Members from another school, or a student on two teams.
  6. Plagiarism, including AI-written work passed off as your own.
  7. The advisor making decisions for the team. Coach P can guide, not decide.
  8. Contacting the case study client.
