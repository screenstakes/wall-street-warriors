Three meetings in a row have failed to decide these. Oct 11 was cancelled. Oct 15 happened and nobody wrote it up. Tonight only three of us turned up, and three cannot carry a plan change.

A ballot fixes the attendance problem. Everyone answers on their own time, the result is written down automatically, and nothing depends on five people being free at once.

Do we adopt a 20% cap on any one theme?

Why this is on the ballot: Five of our nine stocks are one chain: Google and Amazon spend, Nvidia sells the chips, GE Vernova and Eaton supply the power. We have never written down a theme or correlation analysis. A judge finds this in about ninety seconds.

The number is bigger than it looks. The five stocks are 15.52% of the account — but VOO holds those same names for us, another 3.68%. True exposure: 19.20%. Cal State LA's endowment policy caps any one industry at 20% in its own words, and the standard 5/25 rebalancing band on a 16% target gives 12%/20%.

Proposed wording: “No single economic theme may exceed 20% of portfolio value on an index look-through basis. We come back inside the cap by directing new money elsewhere, never by selling a holding whose investment case is intact.”

Your options
  1. Adopt at 20%
  2. Adopt, but at a different number (say it)
  3. No cap — measure and disclose it only
What it costs At 19.20% we are 0.8 of a point under it, so in practice this says we do not add another AI name. It forces no sale today. The reason it is fixed with new money rather than by trimming: a cap that forces selling is backwards, because a breach caused by prices rising would make us sell a winner.

Do we adopt this as our sell rule?

Why this is on the ballot: Right now we have no written rule for when we sell anything, which means every sell would be argued from scratch in the moment — exactly when judgment is worst.

“We sell when the reason we bought no longer describes the company, when a holding breaches a stated weight cap, or when the money is needed for a funding obligation. A fall in price is none of these.”

Three observable conditions, no forecast. Every real IPS we checked — Cal State LA, TWU, the UC endowment — triggers on a weight breach and never on a price move.

Your options
  1. Adopt as written
  2. Adopt with changes (say what)
  3. Reject
What it costs This is the rule that decides Medtronic, so vote on the rule before voting on the stock. It also means we do not sell the AI names because they fell, which some of us may disagree with — say so now rather than in November.
