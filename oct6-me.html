If Coach P says to space the purchases out

He leans that way, and the answer is not commissions — that reason is wrong. It is that lump sum beats averaging in about two times out of three, and we should say so with the numbers.

  • Do not say commissions. It is the reason we gave on the call and it does not hold: nine positions bought once each are $25 apiece whether that happens over one day or three. Only tranching the same position costs more — buying our twelve stock lines in three waves each is 36 trades instead of 12, $900 of commission instead of $300, and 18% of the 200-trade season cap spent on one decision. So if he means buy GEV today and JNJ Thursday, that is free, and we need a better answer than money.
  • The real answer is the evidence. Schwab tested every rolling 20-year period since 1926: investing immediately returned $170,555 against $166,591 for spreading it monthly, and immediate won in 68 of 78 periods. Vanguard gets about the same two-in-three. The reason is unglamorous — markets rise more often than they fall, so time out of the market costs more than a bad entry day does.
  • The number that ends the argument: buying at the exact high every single year for twenty years still returned $151,343 against $47,357 for staying in cash. Worst possible timing beat waiting by more than three times.
  • Give him the counter-argument too, out loud. Spreading really does reduce how much a bad week can hurt us — but less exposed and less invested are the same sentence, and the numbers above are what that sentence costs. Saying the strongest version of his case before he does is the thing criterion 4 grades.
  • And one correction we owe him. I told the team on Sunday that splitting costs extra commission. It does not. The decision to go all at once stands on the evidence, not on the fees, and the minutes should say that.
