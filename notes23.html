Trading Note 1 — Treasury 5.375% of 2031

Pile A. The strongest argument in the whole case, and Wharton's own example note is a Treasury.

Box 1 · the note, exactly as WInS has it

Buying 35 units of the Treasury 5.375% maturing February 2031, 11.87% of the portfolio. It repays a fixed amount on a fixed date, so the money is certain rather than hoped for. Risk: rate moves change its value before maturity. That is how we fund Laura's ten $50,000 payments.

277 characters. Check it against the account before pasting — this is what we meant to type, and Wharton says they verify it.

Box 2 · the reflection

Laura owes ten payments of $50,000 on fixed dates from 2033 to 2042. We matched a fixed obligation with an instrument that behaves the same way. A Treasury note repays a known amount on a known day, so her certainty is structural rather than a probability we estimate. We bought the 2031 rung first because it matures before the payments begin, returning the money in time to buy the later rungs. This is why our strategy splits into piles: the safety pile is sized to the obligation itself, not to a return target.

93 words of the 100 allowed. Covers all three things they ask: why we did it, how it fits the strategy, how it serves Laura.

Trading Note 3 — BIL, the reserve

Pile C. A reserve with a written condition behind it, which most teams cannot defend holding.

Box 1 · the note, exactly as WInS has it

Buying BIL (SPDR 1-3 Month T-Bill ETF) at 13.72% of the portfolio. T-bills hold their value and earn the short rate, so uncommitted money stays ready to buy with. Risk: it barely grows, so money left too long loses to inflation. It is the reserve we deploy on a trigger written in advance.

289 characters. Check it against the account before pasting — this is what we meant to type, and Wharton says they verify it.

Box 2 · the reflection

Most teams cannot defend holding cash, so we gave ours a job. BIL keeps uncommitted money liquid and earning the short rate instead of sitting idle. Laura's binding constraint is reliability, which means we need the ability to act on a decline without selling anything that funds her payments. We also wrote the condition for spending it before we needed to, so deploying would be a rule rather than a reaction. That principle became the part of our IPS that commits us to conditions set in advance instead of judgment in the moment.

93 words of the 100 allowed. Covers all three things they ask: why we did it, how it fits the strategy, how it serves Laura.
