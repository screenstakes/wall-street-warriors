The Nasdaq Dozen

Coach P's twelve fundamental tests, run on ten tech names. Ten of the twelve are scored from real data; two need a ruling from him. The rule we used: more than 6 yes is a maybe-buy, under 6 is probably not, exactly 6 is a judgment call.

10 names · 12 tests

The two highest scorers are AMD at 9 of 10 and Oracle at 8 of 10. AMD keeps 15.58 cents of every sales dollar where Nvidia keeps 63.66, and its stock went up more than 4× in a year on 50% revenue growth. Oracle is burning $45.85B of free cash flow with debt at 252% of equity.

Meanwhile Apple and Alphabet score lowest, at 6. The Dozen has no test for debt, no test for cash flow and no test for margin, so it cannot see the things that actually separate these companies. That is not a knock on the screen. It is what a screen is for: use it to narrow the list, then value what survives.

What the Dozen cannot see

3 gaps
  • DebtOracle scores 8 of 10 with debt at 252% of equity. The only row that even hints at it is the ROE fail, and only because borrowing is what drove ROE from 82% to 41%.
  • Cash flowOracle is burning $45.85B of free cash flow. No test reads a cash flow statement, so that burn costs it nothing on this scorecard.
  • Margin and price levelAMD tops the page at 9 of 10 on a 15.58% margin against Nvidia's 63.66%, at a trailing P/E of 156.79. ROE passing means rising, not high — AMD's rose off a 2.89% base.

Two more things the scoring itself showed. Alphabet's one-time gain passes tests 2 and 5 and then fails test 6, because next year's forecast is lower than the year it inflated — the same event scored twice as good and once as bad. And Apple passes test 4 on a Buy rating whose own average price target, $328.22, sits below the current price. A rating is not the same as an opinion.

So the plan is the plan Coach P gave us, run in order: the Dozen narrows, then we value the survivors with the DCF and required-return work from the Wharton Essentials of Finance material, and only then does anything get a trade card. Of these ten, only Microsoft is on our Pile B shortlist today.

Sources, caveats and what is still open

Where the numbers came from. Tests 1, 2, 5, 6, 8 and 10 from finance.yahoo.com. Test 3 (ROE history) and test 4 (analyst consensus) from stockanalysis.com. Test 11 from Yahoo's insider-transactions page. All pulled .

Three honest caveats.

  • Test 7 is derived, not read off a page: PEG = forward P/E ÷ 5-year growth, so growth = forward P/E ÷ PEG.
  • Test 11 uses Yahoo's 6-month insider window because Yahoo does not publish a 3-month one. The Dozen asks for 3.
  • Yahoo and stockanalysis.com compute PEG differently (0.48 vs 0.36 for Nvidia), so every ratio here is Yahoo's, for consistency.

Two questions for Coach P. Test 9 is ambiguous as the document words it — the title says industry P/E but the text says to pass if earnings beat the industry, and the Walmart example fails for a lower P/E than its industry. Which direction is the pass? And test 12, weighted alpha, is a Barchart metric the document cuts off before defining a pass mark for. Both are marked open on every card above rather than guessed.
