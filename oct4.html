And a second reason, which I only found tonight

WInS enforces a position limit — the most of the portfolio you are allowed to hold in one security. StockTrak's default is 25%, and Wharton sets ours.

BIL at 820 shares is $75,128, which is 25.04%. It is measured against portfolio value, which is $299,655 once commissions come out — so it is really 25.07%. That order does not get a warning. It gets rejected and shows up as System Cancelled.

Even 818 shares fails. 810 clears at 24.77% and costs us about $916 of BIL we did not need. And the check runs when the order executes Monday, not when I click Confirm tonight, so sizing it Monday against real cash is the only way to be sure.

Whytonight works

We can place the orders on the call. They fill at Monday's open.

  • Straight from Wharton's trading page: orders placed while the market is closed fill at the next open. So we do not have to wait for Monday lunch and hope I am free. We key every order at 8:28 with all of us watching, and they execute Monday morning.
  • That matters for more than convenience. Criterion 4 grades our decision process, and “the whole team watched every order go in” is a better answer than “the leader did it alone at lunch”.
  • Place everything except BIL on the call — VOO, VEA, the ten names, both Treasuries. That is $220,783 and it leaves $79,217.
  • I place BIL on Monday, sized to whatever cash is actually there minus a $4,000 buffer. BIL is a three-month Treasury-bill fund — it moves pennies, so there is no cost to waiting a day, and it absorbs the gap whatever the gap turns out to be.
  • Run it forward and the buffer survives everything: a 1% gap leaves 796 shares of BIL and $4,057 cash, a 3% gap leaves 748 shares and $4,046. The buffer never breaks, because BIL is the shock absorber.
