Read this before anything else — why the card matters

You asked what the point of the trade card is if you are just going to place everything anyway. Fair question. The card does two things, and only one of them is voting.

One: it is how a yes gets recorded without a meeting. That is the part you can skip tomorrow, because you are meeting.

Two: it is the graded deliverable. On Oct 23 you submit three Trading Notes from real executed trades plus a 100-word reflection on each. Wharton verifies the trades. The note is what you type into WInS when you place the order, and the card is where the reasoning gets written down while you still remember it. Skip the cards and on Oct 22 you will be reconstructing why you bought NVDA from memory. That is the whole reason it exists.

So tomorrow: the meeting does the voting, and you write the cards as you place. Not before. Not instead.

Your worry, answered

You said you think people will just pick whatever because they do not care. You are half right, and the reason is arithmetic, not attitude. Every name fits. There is no scarcity, so there is nothing to argue about, and people can feel that a vote with no tradeoff is theatre.

So do not pretend it is a cut. Say out loud that everything fits and the only question is whether a name breaks a rule or is obviously wrong. That turns a fake thirty-minute debate into a real ten-minute check, and people engage with it because it is honest.

If you ever want a vote with real stakes, the move is to size by conviction instead of equally — six names at $7,500 and four at $4,500. Do not do that tomorrow. You are behind and demoralised; equal sizing is the right call this week.
