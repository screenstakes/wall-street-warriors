How to key

  • For the nine names and BIL, shares come from Wednesday's price, not from this sheet.

    Pull the quote inside WInS at the moment you key it. Shares = the dollar budget divided by the price, rounded DOWN. Never up — rounding up is how an order comes back cancelled for insufficient cash. VOO 84, VEA 417, the two notes (35 and 37 units) and BIL 492 have their quantity printed: key those exactly as printed. BIL is the only printed number that may go DOWN, and only if cash is short.

  • Key BIL last.

    Everything else has a fixed quantity or a fixed budget. BIL is the line that absorbs the difference, and the only one that may be keyed smaller if cash runs short on Thursday.

  • One login, one person typing.

    Michael keys every order. Nobody else is in the account at the same time.

  • Market orders, every line. Treasuries settle in the evening.

    A blank fill price on a bond is normal until it settles.

  • Keyed after the close, so they fill at the next open.

    The market runs 8:30am to 3:00pm Central. An order keyed in the evening sits queued and executes at Thursday's open. You cannot see the fill price when you key it, which is the one thing that changes — see BIL.
