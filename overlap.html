The rule

Our 5%-at-purchase cap is applied to direct dollars PLUS what VOO carries.

Total = what we key + (the name's VOO weight × $60,010). Divide by $300,000. Under 5% is a ✓.

Adopted in the room on Oct 6. It is why NVIDIA is $9,500 and not $10,000.

VEA overlaps nothing

VEA holds nothing US, so it overlaps none of the nine — checked against all 3,891 names in Vanguard's file.

Medtronic and Eaton look like a trap: both are incorporated in Ireland, so an ex-US fund could hold them. It does not. Both file 10-Ks as US companies, so FTSE Russell's nationality rule treats them as US and the ex-US index leaves them out.

What this does NOT say

  • Overlap is not a flaw.

    An index fund is the market. The nine are the places we chose to hold more than the market does.

  • It does not argue for selling VOO.

    VOO is the core of Pile B. The nine sit on top of it.

  • It is why we count total exposure, not the ticket we type.

    The cap is on what we actually own of a company, through every line.
