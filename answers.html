The thing to notice first

His nine questions are the IPS. Wharton's own instructions ask teams to explain “how the portfolio's asset allocation and composition change as future funding needs approach and payments are made,” how the strategy balances growth, risk, liquidity, funding reliability and financial flexibility, and how it protects the operating commitment. That is his list.

Answer these nine and the 500-word statement is mostly written. He said so himself: being crystal clear will help you develop the IPS.

The number nobody has said out loud

PV of all ten $50,000 payments today, at the 5.18% ten-year: $282,682. Against about $420,814 of contributions. She is roughly 150% funded before we buy anything. Certainty is affordable here; the question is how much to buy.

The gap he spotted

The liability's duration is 11.1 years. Two notes maturing 2032/33 are about 6. That 5-year mismatch is the real defect, and it is worse than owning the wrong asset classes.

What he described has a name

Horizon matching — Leibowitz, 1986. Cash-flow match the near payments, duration-match the far ones. That is exactly “initial funding layer, expanded and maturity-matched over time” in his own message.

1the test

How do we know each pile is doing its job?

  • Pile A — funded ratio. Value of Pile A divided by the present value of the payments still owed. One number, checked monthly. Target 100% by 2033.
  • Pile B — is the surplus growing? Measured against what plain VOO would have done, so we know whether our picking added anything.
  • Pile C — did it actually get used? A flexibility pile that never moves is just drag. If no trigger fires all season, it was too big.
2the hard one

What does “high degree of certainty” mean, and how is it measured?

Proposed: 97% or better, measured two ways that have to agree.

  • Structural. Every payment has a Treasury maturing on or before its date. When that is true the payment is not a forecast, it is a contract.
  • Statistical. Across 20,000 simulated six-year paths, at least 97% leave enough to fund all ten. The current plan comes out at 97.4% — a 2.6% chance of falling short. That is the chart he sent you, with a number on it.
Why state a thresholdThe case makes us define certainty, and the IPS is irrevocable after Nov 6. A rule survives being wrong about a number; a number does not.
3the glide path

Why 25% to Safety, and how should it change?

  • Straight answer: 25% is not “right.” It is the first layer, which is his point and we should just say it.
  • Buying certainty for all ten payments today costs about $282,682, which is roughly two-thirds of her money. We are choosing to fund the near payments and let the rest compound.
  • The rule: add a rung a year. By 2031, when she has to quote co-sponsors, the reserve is substantially matched, so the range we quote is narrow rather than a guess. By 2033 it is fully matched.
Why this matters more than it soundsUnmatched, the 2033 lump sum needed to cover ten payments is anywhere from $402,560 to $500,000 depending on rates. That $97,440 band is what a wide co-sponsor range is made of.
4he called this vague

Why 15% flexible, and what exactly deploys it?

He is right that “for opportunities” is not an answer. Two better ones:

  • Why it exists: so we are never forced to sell Pile A early. Selling a Treasury before maturity is exactly where the price risk lives, and that is the one risk Pile A is built to avoid.
  • Why 15%, and when it changes: the three-month bill pays 4.24%. Holding cash costs almost nothing right now — at Vanguard's equity forecast the six-year cost is between $663 and $11,541. Tie the size to the bill yield, not to habit: 15% while bills pay over 3%, down to 5% if they fall below. At 2021's 0.05% this allocation would have been indefensible.
  • Declared triggers, written before they happen: funding the next ladder rung; rebalancing when a pile drifts 5 points off target; and one named drawdown level. Then a refill rule.
5the trading rules

What decides buy, sell, rebalance, replace?

  • Buy — only against a named pile and a stated job. 3 of 5 approves, 4 of 5 over 10%.
  • Sell — only if the thesis breaks. The breaking condition gets written down at purchase, or it is not a thesis. Price falling is not a reason.
  • Rebalance — checked monthly, acted on at 5 points of drift, funded from Pile C first.
  • Replace — only for a name in the same sector serving the same pile that scores better on the Dozen.
  • Never sell Pile A before maturity except to make a payment.
6the uncomfortable one

How do five stocks improve on the S&P 500 instead of duplicating it?

  • Honest answer: on diversification, they mostly do not. VOO at 30% already owns Microsoft. Five large-cap US names on top of it is an overweight, not a new asset class, and we should not pretend otherwise.
  • So the real reason they are there: Wharton grades our analysis, and we are expressing one specific idea — businesses paid on contracts and subscriptions rather than one-off sales, which mirrors Laura's own need for money that shows up on schedule.
  • The test that follows: if we cannot say how a name behaves differently from the index, it does not go in. That is his “what problem does it solve” applied to equities.
7risks

What are we accepting, and what are we refusing?

Accepting on purpose
  • Equity market risk in Pile B. It is the only thing that grows the surplus.
  • Single-company risk, capped at 3% a name.
  • Currency risk in the international fund.
Refusing on purpose
  • Credit risk in the reserve.
  • Reinvestment risk — we buy maturities, not bond funds.
  • Taiwan concentration, because her project already is that bet.
  • Leverage, shorting, crypto.
8discipline

What would change the strategy, and what would not?

  • Would: the funded ratio dropping below 100%. A rule we wrote turning out to be one we cannot actually follow. A change in what Laura says she needs.
  • Would not: a holding being down. A sector running. Somebody liking a name. A bad week.
  • And after Nov 6, nothing does. Wharton: “Your team may not revise its investment strategy after the submission deadline.” Whatever we write becomes the thing the December report is graded against.
9take back to him

Three things worth raising

  • We cannot buy corporate bonds. Wharton's trading page allows “Treasury bonds from the United States, United Kingdom, Germany, France, Italy, and the Netherlands,” plus any ETF. So the AAA/AA idea can only be done through a fund like QLTA — and a fund never matures, which is the whole thing that makes Pile A certain. Worth asking whether the yield is worth losing the maturity date.
  • His simulated portfolio has 2% HYG in Safety. That is high-yield credit — junk-rated, real default risk — in the pile whose job is funding ten payments with a high degree of certainty. His written advice said AAA/AA. Probably just what the simulator produced, but worth asking.
  • Name what he described. “Initial funding layer, expanded and maturity-matched over time” is horizon matching, Leibowitz 1986: cash-flow match the near payments, duration-match the far ones. Telling him that shows we took the note and went further with it.
