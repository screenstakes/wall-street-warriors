The idea under all of it

Money later is worth less than money now.

Money today can be invested and grow. So every formula does one of two things.

$75.13 today at 10% becomes $100 in 3 years. So $100 in 3 years is worth $75.13 today.

Why it helps us: every number in Laura's plan uses one of these two moves.

Valuing a stock

Price is what you pay. Value is what you get.

A stock's value is all the cash the company will ever make, shrunk back to today:

  1. Guess its free cash flow for 5 years. Shrink each year back, add them up.
  2. Lump every year after into one number, the terminal value. Shrink it back.
  3. Add 1 and 2: the whole business.
  4. Subtract net debt (debt minus cash), divide by shares.

Why it helps us: every trade card needs a number, not a feeling. Call a stock cheap only if value beats price by 20 to 30%.

Valuing a stock · the two guesses

Two guesses decide the whole answer.

Discount rate = 4.5% + beta × 5%4.5% is the 10-year Treasury (check today's). Beta is how hard the stock swings next to the market. Microsoft: 4.5% + 1.13 × 5% ≈ 10%.
Terminal value = year-5 cash × (1 + g) ÷ (rate − g)g is growth after year 5: boring, 2.5% to 4%, always below the rate.

Why it helps us: small guesses swing the answer: Microsoft was $289 at 10% and $350 at 9%. So we pitch a range, and the judges see we know it.
